## C. Argument and clarity

1. **Claim 1 (parity) is stated against the wrong comparator.** The SNN is at parity with the *hand-tuned* opponent (4 of 5 seeds Δ ≤ 0, one significant; one seed significantly worse). But the paper's own classical upper-bound arm is significantly better than that opponent (Δ −0.070 / −0.056) and numerically better than every SNN seed (1.019 / 1.032 vs 1.033–1.146). By the repository's own rule ("every conclusion must be compared with classical control re-tuned at the same budget"), the honest statement is "parity with the hand-tuned schedule, 0.01–0.03 behind the schedule re-tuned with the same ES budget (not tested statistically)". Compute the SNN vs classical + ES paired Δ from the existing rows (see D1) and say which it is. The section title "parity with the re-tuned classical schedule" should name the opponent (hand-tuned, class-wise grid).

2. **Claim 2 ("structural, cannot be met", "unreachable for any controller") is an over-claim.** The audit itself says 0.95 is not physically impossible (per-case oracle 0.87–0.89, dash floor ≈ 0.94). What the data show is: not reached by 25 learned seed-runs and by a classical schedule given the same budget; the metric demands 12–25 % better than the opponent's mean, not 5 %. Rewrite: "The criterion was not reached by any controller we trained, including the classical schedule re-optimised with the same budget; the per-case oracle over all controllers bounds it at 0.87–0.89, so it is reachable only by a controller at the oracle level in every task." Also move the ratio-definition argument (mean of ratios to a median > 1 for right-skewed scores) to one sentence with a reference to the histogram (B, missing figure i).

3. **Claim 3 (robustness, H1): confirmatory vs descriptive is blurred.** Section 2.6 says only the 3 mN·m mid-manoeuvre hit is confirmatory; Section 3.2 then bolds "H1 holds" for nine scenarios and the title says the open-loop schedule "does not" survive (it survives 69 % of the primary cases). Add, once, at the top of 3.2: "One scenario is confirmatory (H1 4/5). The other 14 sets were added by amendment before they ran; their H1 counts are descriptive, uncorrected for 15 scenarios × 5 seeds, and the H1 rule is degenerate when the opponent crashes 0 times (ood, roll-axis: the seed must have 0 crashes) or 192 times (×2: any seed passes)." Un-bold the descriptive rows. Title: "survives more of the disturbances that defeat an open-loop schedule".

4. **Selection of the primary scenario needs one honest sentence.** The robustness tests were designed after the nominal criterion had failed five times (`preregistration_v12.json` says so), and the hit level was calibrated on the opponent to produce moderate crashes. This is legitimate (preregistered before any episode) but a reviewer will call it hypothesis generation after the fact unless the paper says: "the robustness hypothesis was formed after the nominal results and preregistered before any robustness episode; the hit magnitude was chosen on the opponent alone".

5. **Claim 4 (mechanism) rests on a measure whose pass criterion was not preregistered, and the preregistered one failed** (A1-8). The text's logic is nonetheless reasonable (crash rate at matched tilt, pulse count, pitch-axis result), but it is correlational: more pulses after the hit could be a symptom of a larger upset rather than its cure (seed 4 is also the seed with the *largest* tilt at hit). The ablation in D3 would settle it. Until then write "consistent with" rather than "that is where its robustness comes from", and drop the roll-axis test from the argument (A1-11).

6. **An alternative explanation is never addressed: the opponent is handicapped by design.** Section 2.5 states that the opponent's base-law pulses are *suppressed while a schedule segment is active*. During a 0.15–0.45 s hit the opponent therefore has no pulse channel at all (its +0.43 pulses after the hit are from segments that ended), while the SNN keeps it. The "open-loop vs closed-loop" story may partly be "pulses on vs pulses off". One run of the opponent with pulses un-suppressed (D2) is needed before the claim can be made; at minimum state the confound in 3.3 and 3.4.

7. **Imitation-only start: N = 1.** "Robustness comes with the closed-loop layer itself" is inferred from one imitation start (seed 1). Seeds 3–5's starts exist and are frozen (D4). Say "one imitation start (seed 1)" wherever the claim is made.

8. **Seed 2 is counted as an SNN + ES seed but is the imitation start.** This matters twice: in Table R0 (its +0.057 is an imitation-only result) and in every robustness row (its 32 crashes etc. are a second imitation-only arm, which in fact strengthens item 7). Label it "SNN + ES, seed 2 (selection returned the imitation start)" in both tables.

9. **Exploratory seeds.** "best 0.997" reads as a selected best of five; with no archived rows it should not appear in a results table (A1-10). If kept: "mean 1.035 (range 0.997–1.093), approximate machine, files not archived".

## D. Cheap experiments (one 32-core machine, frozen controllers)

1. **SNN + ES vs classical + ES, paired, on the v11 blind set and on the 15 robustness sets.** Question: is the learned layer at parity with the *same-budget* classical, and is its robustness advantage over the re-tuned schedule as large as over the hand-tuned one? Cost: **0 episodes** (rows exist; `paired(cls_es_rows, snn_rows)`).

2. **Opponent with its base-law pulses *not* suppressed during schedule segments**, on the nominal v11 set and the primary kick set. Question: is the opponent's crash rate under the hit due to open-loop timing or to the pulse channel being switched off (C-6)? Cost: 192 + 192 = **384 episodes** (add the 2 mN·m and ×1.5 sets: +384).

3. **Channel ablation of the frozen v11 centres under the primary hit**: (a) learned pulses suppressed, set-points kept; (b) set-points zeroed, pulses kept; seeds 2, 3, 4. Question: does the robustness travel with the pulse channel, as 3.3 claims? Cost: 2 × 3 × 192 = **1 152 episodes** (seed 4 only: 384).

4. **Imitation starts of seeds 3, 4, 5 under the primary hit and under ×1.5 pulses.** Question: is "robust before ES" a property of the architecture or of one start (C-7)? Cost: 3 × 2 × 192 = **1 152 episodes**.

5. **Replication of the primary scenario on a fresh blind base** with the eight main arms (opponent, cls_es 1–2, v11 seeds 1–5). Question: does the only confirmatory result (H1 4/5, with seed 1's CI covering 0 and seed 5's upper bound at −0.02) replicate? Cost: 8 × 192 = **1 536 episodes**. If budget allows, 128 cases per task instead of 64 doubles the power of the paired test (3 072).

## Overall verdict

The numerical core is sound: every number in Table R0, Table R1, the primary-scenario Δ/CIs, the dose–response, the crash table and the mechanism summary reproduces from the archived files, the preregistration/amendment trail is real and the post-hoc demotion is disclosed. The problems are of framing and of a handful of mis-stated details: the "parity" is with the hand-tuned opponent while the authors' own same-budget classical arm beats both; "structurally unreachable" overstates what was shown; nine scenarios are bolded as "H1 holds" although only one is confirmatory and the rule is degenerate at both ends; the mechanism section leans on a measure whose preregistered criterion failed and ignores the most obvious confound (the opponent's pulses are switched off during its schedule); the PPO reference is on a different blind set and is mostly behaviour cloning; the GoogleCloud numbers cannot be checked; and about ten quoted ranges (ES improvement, dash-roll ratio, overshoot, training objectives, per-task crash counts, significant seeds, |Δref|) are wrong by small but visible amounts. A program committee would judge this a careful, honest negative-plus-positive study whose positive claim (fewer crashes under mid-manoeuvre torque hits, 22–40 vs 60 of 192, replicated across hit levels, timing and sustained disturbance) is credible and interesting for a neuromorphic-control venue, but would require (i) the same-budget classical comparator to be the headline comparator, (ii) the pulse-suppression confound to be tested or declared, (iii) the confirmatory/descriptive split to be made explicit in Section 3.2, and (iv) the listed numerical corrections, before acceptance; with D1–D3 run (≈ 1 500 episodes) the mechanism claim could be made as a result rather than a consistency argument.
