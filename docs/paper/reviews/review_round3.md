## 3. Framing: the pulses-on opponent and the mechanism conclusion

1. **Status is stated clearly and honestly.** 2.5, 3.0, 3.2 and 3.4 all say it was added after a review of this draft, run after all other results were known, was not part of any criterion, and that every H1 verdict is relative to the weaker opponent. Good. Two precision fixes: the "every set" preregistration wording (item 1.1-14), and 3.0's "three classical references" should also name which one is the imitation teacher and ES normaliser (the preregistered one).

2. **"Upper bound" is now contradicted by the data and must go.** 2.5 heads the classical + ES arm "Upper-bound arm", 2.6 says "two upper-bound centres", 2.5's section title says "classical upper bound", and Fig. R6 labels it "upper bound". The pulses-on opponent beats it nominally (0.955 vs 1.019 / 1.032; Δ −0.134 vs −0.070 / −0.056) and on every robustness set. Rename to "same-budget re-optimised schedule (classical + ES)" everywhere and say in 2.5 why it is no longer an upper bound.

3. **0.955 is within 0.005 of the preregistered threshold.** The pulses-on opponent, with 0 crashes, essentially meets the criterion (≤ 0.95, crashes ≤ opponent) that 25 learned training runs and the ES-tuned schedule failed; 3.1 buries this in "none reaches 0.95". A program committee will see it immediately; say it in one sentence in 3.1 and in 3.4 bullet 1 ("a one-line change to the baseline comes within 0.005 of the criterion").

4. **The pulses-on arm was not re-tuned** — its schedules were tuned with pulses off (2.5). It could be stronger still. State this as part of 3.4 bullet 1; it also means "the best classical design available on this vehicle" is still unknown.

5. **3.3 under-claims the comparison.** "a hand-designed law that keeps pulsing is at least as robust" — it is much more robust: 5 vs 22–40 crashes on the primary set, 4 vs 16–39 on the replication, 0 vs 2–9 at 2 mN·m, 7 vs 22–45 early; the SNN seeds are +1.1 … +2.0 behind it in paired Δ on the primary set (all intervals above 0, recomputed). Write "substantially more robust"; the learned layer re-discovered the mechanism but implements it worse than the PLL law (3-tick spike-triggered twitches vs phase-locked windows).

6. **Is the 3.3 conclusion supported?** Yes, with two nuances the text should carry. (a) nopulse (62–93 crashes, ≥ the opponent's 60) shows the pulse channel is *necessary* for the learned stack's robustness; pulses-on (5) shows a pulse channel during segments is *sufficient* in the classical stack; together they support "robustness comes from keeping the phase-locked pulse channel active while the attitude is commanded away from hover". (b) noref (33–54) crashes **7–22 more than the full centres (22–40)** while still below the opponent — the set-point channel contributes too; "The ablations make this causal within the learned stack" should read "both channels contribute, the pulse channel dominates". (c) A caveat a reviewer will raise: the ablation removes the channel for the whole manoeuvre, not only for the post-hit reaction, so the nopulse arm may already be in a different state when the hit lands (its tilt-at-hit was not traced); the "more pulses after the hit" measure (3.3) is what ties the effect to the reaction. Say so in one clause. (d) "the imitation-only networks already have it" deserves the supporting fact from 2.4: the teacher's pulse label is `pulse and not active`, i.e. the teacher never pulsed during a segment, so the imitation networks' pulsing during manoeuvres is generalisation from hover, not copied behaviour.

7. **3.2 "H1 holds" vs 3.4 "opponent mis-specified".** Not formally contradictory because 3.4 scopes every verdict to the preregistered opponent, but the bold "**H1 holds (4 of 5)**" in the primary paragraph of 3.2 is read before the pulses-on paragraph, and Fig. R2's "H1 4/5" labels sit next to a hatched bar at 2.6 %. Add one clause to the primary paragraph: "relative to the preregistered opponent; the pulses-on variant crashes 5 times on the same cases (below)". The same applies to the 3.1 heading "Parity with the hand-tuned opponent": "hand-tuned" is now also an accurate description of the pulses-on arm; use "preregistered opponent" there.

8. **Only one place where the learned layer beats the best classical reference: ×1.5.** 3.2 and 3.3 both state it; neither says the comparison was one of the three preregistered d2 sets (it was), nor offers any mechanism (the ×1.5 disturbance is a 0.2 s force+torque pulse landing 0.2–0.4 s into the manoeuvre, i.e. the same timing as the primary hit but lower torque and sustained — the one case where a learned IMU-driven reaction might beat a fixed phase window). Say "preregistered" and either give a one-sentence hypothesis or say none is offered.

9. **Over-claims elsewhere:** none serious. "ES adds speed and, without crash-aware ranking, removes margin" (3.2) is supported by the v9 range vs the v11 centres. **Under-claim:** 3.4 bullet 1 should state the pulses-on nominal advantage per task (evade −0.08, redirect −0.27, dash −0.05 from `summary_d1_d4.json`): it is mostly a redirect effect, which is also where the SNN's nominal advantage over the preregistered opponent lies (−0.12 … −0.19), so the two are plausibly the same mechanism.

## Verdict

The numbers are now almost entirely right (every table entry, every CI in Table R0/R1, the primary, replication, dose–response, ablation and pulses-on results reproduce from the archived files), the round-2 experiments were run and preregistered, and the post-hoc status of the pulses-on opponent is disclosed honestly; the remaining errors are a dozen small mis-stated ranges and two inherited mis-readings (tilt-bin overlap, "two" v9 seeds), plus one broad over-claim about the SNN vs classical + ES on "every" set.
