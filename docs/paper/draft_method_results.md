## 2. Method

### 2.1 Platform and interface

**Vehicle and simulator.** The vehicle is the IST_robot-V2 flapping-wing micro air vehicle (15 g, one motor driving two wings at ≈26.7 Hz, i.e. a 37 ms wingbeat; IMU on the lower body). It is simulated in MuJoCo 3.8.0 as a multibody system with blade-element aerodynamics whose lift is calibrated against bench measurements of the real vehicle. The controller runs at 500 Hz (2 ms tick); the plant integrates at 200 kHz (400 physics sub-steps per tick).

**Interface (unchanged throughout).** At every tick the controller receives the 11-dimensional sensor vector `[roll, pitch, yaw (rad), gx, gy, gz (rad/s), ax, ay, az (m/s²), z (m), vz (m/s)]`: attitude and rates from the IMU with Gaussian noise (0.02°, 0.002 rad/s, 0.02 m/s²), `z` from a time-of-flight range finder (50 Hz, 20 ms latency, 2 mm noise) and `vz` from an α-β observer. It commands the two gimbal angles α, β ∈ ±0.3 rad and the motor speed ω ∈ [3115, 3807] rad/s; the commands are updated every 2 ms and pass through an 8.2 ms first-order actuator lag. Measured hover needs ≈3140 rad/s, only ≈25 rad/s above the lower limit, so downward thrust authority is small. The sensor set, actuator set, update rate and limits (`LOW`/`HIGH`, `DT`, the untouched `ControlEnv`) are shared by every controller in this paper; new test scenarios only change the case definitions (Section 2.6).

**Stress condition.** All training, validation and blind cases are "stress" cases: initial attitude error ±5°, motor gain uncertainty ±1.5 %, accelerometer bias 0.01 m/s² and attitude bias 0.05°, one control step (2 ms) of sensor delay, and periodic disturbance pulses: a force of 0.006 N and a torque of 8·10⁻⁶ N·m, switched on for 0.2 s whenever `t mod 2 s ∈ [0.6, 0.8) s` (i.e. at 0.6, 2.6 and 4.6 s) with a random sign per episode, so one pulse lands 0.2–0.4 s into every redirect and dash manoeuvre. Each case is one random seed; eight strata fix the wing state at release (the wingbeat phase at take-off). Episodes last 5 s (evade) or 6.5 s (redirect, dash).

### 2.2 Manoeuvre tasks and scoring

Three manoeuvres are commanded during hover (Fig. M5, `fig_m5_tasks.png`). Directions are given in the heading frame fixed at the command onset `t0` (yaw at `t0`; 0° = forward, 90° = left); the measured attitude-to-motion sign map is (roll, pitch)_ref ∝ (−sin φ, +cos φ). The command is a unit vector (cos φ, sin φ) on one of two channels (evade; dash/redirect) and is presented to the learned layer as eight rectified inputs (± of each component on each channel). The command switches off when the goal is detected on the ground-truth position.

| task | command | goal | window | classes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **evade** | displace 0.10 m along φ ∈ {0, 90, 180, 270}°; `t0 ~ U(2.2, 2.8) s` | reach 0.10 m as fast as possible | 1.5 s | pitch (0/180°), roll (90/270°) |
| **redirect** | go along φ₁ ∈ {0, 90, 180, 270}°; when 0.08 m or 1.0 s is reached the command switches to φ₂ = φ₁ ± 90°; `t0 = 2.4 s` | reach 0.10 m along φ₂ after the switch with little overrun along φ₁ | 4 s | by φ₂: pitch, roll |
| **dash** | displace 0.15 m along φ ∈ {0, 45, …, 315}° and stop; `t0 = 2.4 s` | reach, then settle (speed along φ < 0.1 m/s for 0.3 s), little overshoot | 4 s | pitch, roll, diagonal |

**Score (lower is better).** With `W` the window, `short` the shortfall to the goal distance (m), `cross` the lateral position error at the reach time (evade) or at the end of the window (dash), `max|Δz|` the largest altitude deviation (cm) from the altitude at `t0` over the rest of the flight (symmetric; plan v9 onwards), and `crash = 10 + 5·(unflown fraction of the window)` if the episode terminated early (else 0):

- evade: `(t_reach, or 1.5 + 10·short) + 0.5·|cross| + 0.03·max|Δz| + crash`;
- redirect: `t_switch + (t_reach2, or (4 − t_switch) + 10·short) + 2·max(0, overrun − 0.05) + 0.03·max|Δz| + crash`, where `overrun` is the displacement along φ₁ after the switch; a case that never switches scores `4 + 10·0.10 + …`;
- dash: `(t_reach, or 4 + 2·short) + (t_settle, or 4) + 2·max(0, max_along − 0.25) + 0.5·|cross| + 0.03·max|Δz| + crash`.

A **crash** is any early termination: true tilt > 80.2°, |z − z_start| > 1 m, absolute height z < 0.12 m, any body part within 3 cm of the ground, or a numerical failure. Tilt is the true angle between the body axis and the vertical (acos R₃₃ from the simulator state).

**Ratio.** For comparability across tasks and directions, each case score is divided by the median score of the classical opponent over the blind cases of the same direction class (32 cases per class for evade and redirect; 16/16/32 for dash pitch/roll/diagonal); the task ratio is the mean over cases and the *manoeuvre ratio* is the mean over the three tasks. Because the score distributions are right-skewed, this ratio is above 1 even for the opponent itself (1.089 on the plan-v11 blind set, Section 3.1). The main comparisons therefore use the **paired difference** Δ = (arm ratio − opponent's own ratio) on identical cases, with a paired bootstrap 95 % interval over cases within task (4000 resamples), the paired win count, and crash counts with exact Clopper–Pearson 95 % intervals.

### 2.3 Hierarchical controller

The controller has three layers (Fig. M1, `fig_m1_architecture.png`; one 2 ms tick in Fig. M3, `fig_m3_gating.png`).

**Reflex layer — PID.** A PID attitude loop drives the gimbal: `e = ref − [roll, pitch]`, derivative on the measurement through a 4.5 ms low-pass, integral clipped to ±0.3 with anti-windup, `[α, β] = trim + [effort_pitch, effort_roll]`; a PID altitude loop sets the motor speed (gains 900, 500, 80 on z error, vz and ∫z). The attitude gains were re-tuned on stress hover (kp × 1.5, kd × 0.85, ki × 1.5 relative to the original baseline); a full-state-feedback upper bound improves the hover tilt RMS by only 1.1°, so no learned component touches hovering.

**Steering-muscle layer — phase-locked motor pulses.** A motor pulse drives the motor command to its maximum (3807 rad/s) for a few milliseconds; after the 8.2 ms actuator lag it raises the speed by ≈690 rad/s during part of a wingbeat, and the resulting lift asymmetry within the stroke produces a **pitch torque** whose sign depends on the wingbeat phase of the pulse. The classical pulse law (the base law of every arm, active outside manoeuvre commands) is `u = −(k_θ·pitch + k_q·q_slow)` with k_θ = 8.0, k_q = 0.4, `q_slow` the pitch rate with the wingbeat harmonics notched out, and a PLL estimate of the crank phase; it holds the maximum command while `|u| > 0.5` and the phase is within ±22.5° of 135° (u > 0) or 315° (u < 0), i.e. ≈4.6 ms per wingbeat at most. The learned layer's pulses are 3-tick (6 ms) twitches triggered by output spikes (next paragraph).

**Descending layer — the spiking network** (Fig. M2, `fig_m2_snn.png`). A leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) network with axonal delays:

- input `x_t ∈ ℝ²⁵` per tick: eight IMU channels (roll, pitch, gx, gy, gz, ax, ay, az) scaled by 0.1 rad, 10 rad/s and 25 m/s² respectively and split into rectified + and − channels (16), a constant 1 (bias), and the eight rectified command channels;
- 11 input delay taps `k = 0…10` ticks (0–20 ms): hidden drive `I_h(t) = Σ_k W_in[:, :, k] x_{t−k} + b_h`, `W_in ∈ ℝ^{8×25×11}`;
- 8 hidden LIF neurons updated in two 1 ms sub-steps per tick as unit-gain first-order filters, `c ← a_s c + (1 − a_s) I`, `v ← a_m v + (1 − a_m) c`, `a = exp(−1 ms / τ)`, spike when `v ≥ 1` followed by subtractive reset; τ_m and τ_s are learned in the imitation stage and frozen afterwards (selected centres: τ_m = 1.9–2.1 ms, τ_s = 1.05–1.15 ms);
- hidden → output through 11 axonal delays `d = 0, 2, …, 20 ms` on the hidden spike trains `s`, with a fixed spike scale of 5: `I_o = 5·Σ_d W_out[:, :, d] s_{t−d} + b_o` into two output LIF neurons of the same form (`b_o` initialised to −2), and `I_g = 5·Σ_d W_g[:, :, d] s_{t−d} + b_g` into two graded units `g ← a_g g + (1 − a_g) c_g`, τ_g = 20 ms;
- outputs: a spike of either output neuron triggers one 3-tick motor twitch (two output neurons are two routes to the same twitch; the timing within the wingbeat carries the meaning); the graded units give the **roll and pitch set-points** `ref = 0.7 rad · tanh(g)` handed to the PID;
- 2564 parameters in total (`W_in`, `b_h`, `W_out`, `b_o`, `W_g`, `b_g`); the network has no notion of height or of time since the command (plan v10 tested 12 extra inputs for these and found no gain).

**Command gate and safety rules.** The learned layer is in charge only while a command is on and for a 1 s tail after it ends; outside the gate the base pulse law acts and the set-points are zero, so every arm flies identically tick for tick before `t0` (checked bit-exactly: the prefix cache of Section 2.4 reproduces the plain episode to the last bit, and in the pre-command-hit scenario of Section 3.3 all arms had identical pre-`t0` traces on 192/192 cases). While in charge: pulse onsets are separated by a refractory period of at least 37 ms, and the righting reflex is off. Outside the gate, if the sensed tilt exceeds 25° the base law is replaced for the rest of the episode by a righting pulse law (k_θ = 3.0, k_q = 0.4, threshold 0.5, 90° window). The learned layer can act no earlier than 1.5 s after take-off (before that its set-points are zeroed and its pulses suppressed).

### 2.4 Training

The pipeline (Fig. M4, `fig_m4_pipeline.png`) has three stages; the controller that is tested is the frozen output of stage 3. All stages run in the stress condition on disjoint seed ranges (training, validation and blind sets never share a seed).

**Stage 1 — imitation of the classical schedule.** The classical opponent (Section 2.5) flies 16 training and 4 validation cases per task. From `t = 2 s` on, every tick records the 25-D input, whether the opponent fired a pulse (its base-law pulses are suppressed while a schedule segment is active, so the teacher fires only outside active segments), the sign of its law variable `u` (the nose-up / nose-down label), and its set-points. A torch twin of the network with identical tick arithmetic is trained by back-propagation through time with a sigmoid surrogate gradient (slope 1/0.25) on windows of 200 ticks (stride 100, batches of 32 windows). The loss is `mean[(1 + 2y)·BCE(p, y)] + 5·mean[(ref/0.7 − r)²]`, where `y` is the recorded pulse label, `p = max over the two sub-steps of σ((v_o − 1)/0.25)` is the surrogate firing probability of the output neuron of the labelled sign, and `r` the recorded set-points; Adam 3·10⁻³, gradient norm clipped to 1, 40 epochs, best epoch by validation loss. Five seeds give five imitation starts, shared by all plans from v7 on.

**Stage 2 — OpenAI-ES on the manoeuvre score.** Starting from the imitation weights θ₀ (the six weight/bias tensors; the time constants are frozen), each generation draws 8 antithetic pairs (population 16) of perturbations ε ~ N(0, I), evaluates θ + σε and the centre θ on the generation's batch, and updates θ with rank-based utilities (`u_i ∝ max(0, log(9) − log(rank_i + 1))`, centred), the gradient estimate `Σ u_i ε_i / σ` normalised by Adam moments (0.9, 0.999, bias-corrected), σ = 0.02, learning rate 0.01 · 0.85^g, 16 generations (random seed 1000 + seed). The objective of a candidate is the mean over tasks of the mean over its cases of `score / N_{task,class}`, where `N` is the opponent's class mean on 24 fixed normalisation cases per task, plus a soft tilt penalty of 0.3 per degree of the tilt peak above 60°. Two features distinguish the final protocol (plan v11) from the earlier runs (plans v7–v10):

- *full coverage*: 8 cases per task per generation, which covers all 8 dash directions, all 8 redirect pairs (both turn directions), all 4 evade directions and all 8 release strata every generation (the earlier 4-case batches used fixed direction slots — dash 0/45/90/135° and one turn direction only — and only strata 0–3, a defect found in plan v10);
- *crash-aware ranking*: any candidate with a crashed episode ranks below every crash-free candidate.

A generation costs 17 × 24 = 408 episodes (6 528 per seed). The population shares the pre-command prefix of each case through a bit-exact environment snapshot (`cache8`: a deep copy of the simulator state with the network state rebuilt by replaying the recorded inputs; verified to reproduce the plain episode bit for bit), so only the post-command part is simulated per candidate.

**Stage 3 — selection.** The imitation start and the centre of every odd generation (9 centres) fly a fixed validation set of 64 cases per task (192 cases); centres with any crash or a tilt peak ≥ 70° are excluded and the centre with the lowest objective is selected. The selected centre is frozen; it is the only network that is ever evaluated on a blind set.

### 2.5 Classical opponent, pulses-on variant and same-budget re-optimised schedule

**Opponent.** The strongest classical scheme with the same actuators, PID and base law: an open-loop 2-D set-point schedule per task and direction class — go set-point magnitude along φ, go duration, brake set-point magnitude (reversed) and brake duration, durations in 40 ms beats — started by the command, restarted on a direction change (redirect) and ended by the command drop; base-law pulses are suppressed while a segment is active. Each schedule was tuned on its own grid and tuning cases (evade and redirect: 24 configurations × 8 cases per task; dash: 108 configurations × 16 cases, inherited from plan v6), best configuration per class by mean score. No learned component is involved. The opponent also provides the class medians of the ratio and the normalisers of the ES objective.

**Pulse suppression and the "pulses-on" variant (plan v14).** The opponent, as preregistered, suppresses its base-law pulses while a schedule segment is active (`pulse and not active`), a design choice made when the schedules were tuned. A reviewer of this draft asked whether the learned layer's robustness could simply be "pulses on vs pulses off"; we therefore also report the same schedules with the base-law pulses kept on (`ClassicalUnsuppressed`; one changed line, no re-tuning — the schedules were tuned with the pulses off), run after all other results were known: preregistered as plan v14 on three sets (the nominal set, the primary hit and the ×1.5 disturbance) and extended by amendment to every other set after those three results were seen and before those episodes ran. This variant was *not* part of any criterion and is treated as an additional classical reference.

**Same-budget re-optimised schedule — classical + ES (plan v11, amendment 1).** To test whether the score leaves room above the opponent at all, the 28 schedule parameters (7 classes × 4) were optimised with exactly the ES budget of stage 2 — population 16, 16 generations, the same 24-episode batches with full coverage, crash-aware ranking — with σ = 0.05 and learning rate 0.02 in normalised parameter units (set-points divided by 0.5 rad, beat counts by 20), selected on the same 192 validation cases; four seeds on three machines. It was conceived as a classical upper bound; the pulses-on opponent is better than it on every blind set (Section 3), so it is reported only as the same-budget comparator.

### 2.6 Evaluation protocol

Every plan was preregistered: tasks, scoring, arms, blind-set seed base, number of cases and the pass criterion were committed to the repository before any blind episode (e.g. plan v11: commit `47d79a0`, before its first training episode; plan v12: `0726527`; plan v13 amendment 2: `abb78bc`), and amendments were committed before the episodes they cover; the one post-hoc amendment (`plan_v12/amendment_2_post_hoc.json`) demotes the plant-mismatch scenario from a primary to a secondary claim after its result was seen and is reported as such. Blind sets use seed bases never used before; each has 64 cases per task (192 episodes per arm) with the same direction and stratum coverage as the validation set. The classical opponent flies every blind case, so every comparison is paired. The reference machine (CloudPC-2, 32 vCPU) and a second machine (HomePC) produce identical episodes (0 differences in score, pulses and duration over 192 opponent episodes); a third machine (GoogleCloud) does not reproduce the reference bit for bit (floating-point differences amplified by the dynamics) and was used only for exploratory seeds that are reported separately and never counted in a criterion.

**Robustness scenarios (plans v12–v13; frozen controllers, no training).** The selected v11 centres (5 seeds), the v9 centres (5 seeds), the imitation start of seed 1, the opponent and the two classical + ES centres were flown, unchanged, on fresh blind sets in which one element of the case is changed (Table R1 in Section 3):

- a 20 ms torque hit about a horizontal axis fixed in the heading frame (an external torque added to the body in the simulator), at a random time during the manoeuvre (`t0 + U(0.15, 0.45) s`; also early `+0–0.15 s`, late `+0.5–1.0 s`, before the command `−0.6…−0.3 s`, a double hit 80–200 ms apart, and hits restricted to the roll or the pitch axis); magnitudes 2, 3 and 4.5 mN·m, where 3 mN·m was fixed before any learned-arm episode as the largest level at which the opponent crashes on ≤ 20 % of 24 calibration cases;
- the periodic disturbance pulses of the stress condition scaled ×1.25 and ×1.5 (×2 and ×3 were also run and lie beyond what any controller survives: all arms crash on nearly every case);
- sensor degradation: attitude and gyro noise ×5 (0.10°, 0.010 rad/s); sensor delay 3 steps (6 ms) instead of 1;
- plant mismatch: lower-body mass and inertia +12.5 % (a pre-existing out-of-distribution condition of the environment).

The preregistered criterion per scenario (H1) is: a v11 seed passes when its paired-Δ interval lies below 0 and its crashes do not exceed the opponent's; the scenario "holds" when ≥ 3 of 5 seeds pass. The 3 mN·m mid-manoeuvre hit is the primary (confirmatory) scenario, formulated after the nominal criteria had failed and calibrated on the opponent only; the other scenarios were added by amendments before they ran and are reported descriptively with the same rule, without correction for multiplicity (17 blind sets were flown; 15 descriptive ones besides the primary scenario and its replication, each with 5 seeds). "Wins" counts cases with a strictly lower score than the opponent. The primary scenario was replicated once on a fresh seed base (plan v14). Plan v14 also added, after a review of this draft and before running: the pulses-on opponent on the nominal set, the primary hit and the ×1.5 disturbance (then on every other set by amendment, Section 2.5); channel ablations of the frozen v11 centres under the primary hit (pulses suppressed while in charge; set-points zeroed while in charge); and the imitation starts of seeds 2–5 under the primary hit and the ×1.5 disturbance.

## 3. Results

Three classical references appear throughout and are named consistently: the **preregistered opponent** (hand-tuned schedules, base-law pulses suppressed while a segment is active; the imitation teacher, the ES normaliser, the comparator of every criterion and the normaliser of every ratio), **classical + ES** (the same schedules re-optimised with the ES budget, Section 2.5) and the **pulses-on opponent** (the preregistered schedules with the base-law pulses kept on, plan v14, added after a review of this draft). Three normalisations occur: the ES batch objective (class *means* of the opponent on 24 fixed cases, plus the tilt penalty), the selection objective (the same normaliser on the 192 validation cases) and the blind ratio (class *medians* of the opponent on the blind cases); they differ by 0.03–0.05 and are not interchangeable. Unless stated otherwise, Δ is the paired difference to the preregistered opponent on identical cases and crashes are counts out of 192.

### 3.1 Nominal manoeuvres

**The preregistered ratio criterion was not met by any controller we trained.** Plans v7–v11 each asked, per seed, for a manoeuvre ratio ≤ 0.95 with no more crashes than the opponent, in at least 3 of 5 seeds (Fig. R0). Plan v11, the final protocol, gives 1.046 / 1.146 / 1.033 / 1.054 / 1.063 for seeds 1–5 (Table R0) with **0 crashes in 960 blind episodes**; the earlier plans gave 0.97–1.07 with 10 crashes (opponent 5; plan v8, the v7 controllers re-tested on 64 cases per task), 1.02–1.13 with 3 crashes (opponent 0; v9) and 1.02–1.25 with 6 crashes (opponent 3; v10). The v7 "full" values of 0.89–1.00 (2 crashes, opponent 1) were obtained on 32 cases per task on a blind set where the opponent's own ratio was 1.18; re-tested on 64 fresh cases the same five controllers scored 0.97–1.07.

The ratio is above 1 for the opponent itself — 1.089 on the v11 blind set, 1.086 on v9, 1.273 on v10, 1.415 on v8 — because the per-case ratios are right-skewed (Fig. R0b: medians near 1, a tail of slow or crashed cases up to 3). Classical + ES reaches 1.019 / 1.032 on the bit-identical machines (0.999 / 1.018 on the approximate one) and the pulses-on opponent 0.955, all with 0 crashes; none reaches 0.95, but the pulses-on opponent — a one-line change to the baseline — comes within 0.005 of the preregistered criterion that no learned controller met. An AI-assisted re-analysis of the per-case rows of plans v7–v10 (`plan_v11/audit/audit_report.md`, done before the v11 results) bounds the metric at 0.87–0.89 even when the best of the 5–14 available controllers is taken case by case, and in the dash task no controller met the settle condition in a single pitch- or diagonal-class episode (0 of ≈1 800 in v9–v10; 0 of all v11 episodes for all seven arms), so a constant 4 s term enters both numerator and denominator of that cell.

For reference, a MLP + PPO layer trained on the same tasks (plan v8, 5 seeds, three of which selected their imitation start) scored 1.109 ± 0.024 with 1 crash in 960 episodes on the v8 blind set, where the SNN + ES centres scored 0.97–1.07 with 10 crashes and the opponent crashed 5 times.

**Primary scenario and its replication.** With a 3 mN·m hit landing 0.15–0.45 s into the manoeuvre the opponent crashes in 60 of 192 cases, the re-optimised schedules in 56–58, the v11 SNN centres in 22–40 and the imitation starts in 23–32. The paired Δ of the five seeds is −0.125 [−0.426, +0.209], −0.282 [−0.448, −0.121], −0.355 [−0.540, −0.186], −0.584 [−0.815, −0.377] and −0.263 [−0.473, −0.024]: **H1 holds (4 of 5)**, relative to the preregistered opponent (the pulses-on variant, below, crashes 5 times on the same cases). On a fresh seed base the result replicates (42 vs 16–39 crashes; Δ −0.09 … −1.05; 4 of 5, seed 1 failing both times). Per task, the gain is in dash (2–10 crashes vs 19) and redirect (12–22 vs 24); evade crashes less too (6–13 vs 17) but its Δ has no consistent sign.

**Dose–response, timing, axis, repetition (Fig. R4).** At 2 mN·m the SNN crashes 2–9 times against 17 (Δ −0.57 … −0.89, all seeds); at 4.5 mN·m the opponent crashes in 45 % of the cases, the SNN in 24–49 %, and only seed 4 remains significantly better (Δ −0.65 [−0.94, −0.40]; seed 1 is worse, +0.40 [+0.00, +0.79]). The advantage shrinks once the hit exceeds what the learned layer's own tilt margin (the distance from its operating tilt to the 80° crash threshold) can absorb, as anticipated, with a hedge, in the amendment. The amendment on hit timing expected the largest advantage for early hits; the opposite was found: late hits (0.5–1.0 s, during braking and settling, with the learned layer still in charge through its 1 s tail) give 5–16 crashes against 40, all seeds passing, whereas early hits (0–0.15 s, during the initial lean) are the hardest for everyone (56 vs 22–45). A hit 0.3–0.6 s before the command produces bit-identical trajectories up to `t0` for all arms (192/192 cases; checked by comparing, per case, the number of pre-`t0` ticks and the sums of the attitude, position and velocity columns of the trace), after which the learned layer handles the command arriving during recovery with 1–4 crashes against 7. Hits about the roll axis crash the opponent 0 times and the other arms 0–2 times, except v11 seed 1 (17 crashes; Δ +0.84 [+0.42, +1.32]), so this scenario cannot separate the arms; hits about the pitch axis crash the opponent in 74 cases and the SNN in 36–51, in line with the random-axis result. A second hit 80–200 ms after the first reduces the margin again (84 vs 44–73, 2 of 5).

**Sustained disturbance, sensors, plant.** (The ×1.5 set was one of the three on which the pulses-on opponent was preregistered.) With the periodic disturbance pulses scaled ×1.5 the opponent crashes 40 times and the re-optimised schedules 36–38, the v11 centres 5–9 and the five imitation starts 3–8 (Δ −0.87 … −1.03, all seeds); at ×1.25, 10 vs 0–2. Five-fold IMU noise harms neither side (0–3 crashes; Δ −0.12 … −0.24, one seed's interval excluding 0). Tripling the sensor delay harms the opponent more than the learned layer (9 and 11–12 vs 0–4; Δ −0.33 … −0.45, 4 of 5), against the expectation stated before the run. With +12.5 % body mass nothing crashes and the SNN is slightly but significantly *worse* for three seeds (Δ +0.047 [+0.027, +0.067], +0.070 [+0.046, +0.094], +0.021, +0.040 [+0.005, +0.076], +0.017); this is the one scenario with a negative result for the learned layer and was demoted to secondary after the result was seen.

**The pulses-on opponent.** Keeping the base-law pulses on during the schedule segments — one changed line in the preregistered opponent, no re-tuning — changes the picture: this variant crashes 5 times under the primary hit, 4 in the replication and 30 at 4.5 mN·m (Table R1 gives every set), with Δ −0.09 … −1.77 to the preregistered opponent on every set and −0.134 nominally. In every hit scenario its crash count is at or below that of every v11 seed except the late-hit set (7 vs 5–16; no seed significantly better in paired Δ); the one set on which the learned layer is significantly better is the ×1.5 sustained disturbance (5–9 vs 24; Δ −0.9 … −1.1 to the pulses-on rows, all intervals below 0), one of the three sets on which this arm was preregistered. We offer no mechanism for that exception; the ×1.5 disturbance is a 0.2 s force-plus-torque pulse landing 0.2–0.4 s into the manoeuvre, i.e. a lower, sustained torque at the timing of the primary hit, where an IMU-driven reaction may beat a fixed phase window. Classical + ES, which inherits the suppression, gains no robustness (16 / 56–58 / 87–89 crashes across the hit levels). Relative to classical + ES the SNN seeds are better on the 2 and 3 mN·m hits, the early, late and pitch-axis hits and both disturbance levels (Δ −0.2 … −1.6, most intervals below 0), mixed at 4.5 mN·m and under the double, pre-command and roll-axis hits (seed 1 worse on three of them, seed 2 on the pre-command hit), indistinguishable on the sensor sets and slightly worse under plant mismatch (+0.02 … +0.08, three intervals above 0).

**Older centres and the imitation starts.** The plan-v9 centres (trained without full coverage and crash-aware ranking) span the whole range in the primary scenario (26–52 crashes; Δ −0.53 … +0.50, one seed significantly worse than the opponent, +0.50 [+0.02, +1.06], and one numerically worse, +0.18 [−0.13, +0.54]), whereas all five v11 centres lie below it. The five imitation starts are as robust as the ES centres (23–32 crashes under the primary hit; 3–8 at ×1.5), so the robustness is a property of the architecture-plus-imitation stack rather than of ES fine-tuning; ES adds speed and, without crash-aware ranking, removes margin.

### 3.3 Mechanism: an active pulse channel during the manoeuvre

Three analyses, all on the 192 primary-scenario cases (Fig. R5; `plan_v13/mechanism_summary.json`, `plan_v14/summary_d1_d4.json`), converge on one explanation.

*Not a smaller tilt margin.* The learned arms do not fly at a smaller tilt when the hit lands: the mean true tilt at the hit is 14.6° [13.7, 15.5] for the opponent and 13.9–16.9° for the SNN seeds (seed 4, the most robust, +2.4° [+1.7, +3.0]). At matched tilt-at-hit the SNN crashes less in the < 20° bin (122–166 cases per arm): 11–17 % against 31 % [24, 39] for the opponent; in the 20–35° bin (26–67 cases) every interval overlaps the opponent's (seed 4: 12 % [5, 22] vs 31 % [16, 48]). The tilt peak within 0.5 s after the hit is lower for three of the four learned arms (−4.9° [−5.8, −4.0], −2.0° [−3.3, −0.8], −5.6° [−6.5, −4.7]; seed 3 −0.1°).

*The preregistered set-point criterion is not met.* The test registered "reaction" as a set-point change within 150 ms that opposes the induced tilt, where the induced tilt is the change of the true (roll, pitch) vector over the 60 ms after the hit, a change is "corrective" when its projection on that vector is negative, and the projection is the dot product of the 150 ms set-point change with the unit induced-tilt vector. The learned arms change their set-points by 0.19–0.39 rad over that window (opponent 0.25 rad, its schedule continuing) but the change is not systematically against the induced tilt: the projection on the induced-tilt direction is ≥ 0 on average for every arm, and the change is corrective in 32–51 % of cases against 14 % for the opponent. The set-point route therefore does not explain the robustness.

*The pulse channel does.* In the 150 ms after the hit the learned arms fire 1.1–3.6 more motor pulses than in the 150 ms before (seed 4: +3.58 [+3.0, +4.2]); the opponent fires +0.43 because its base-law pulses are suppressed while a segment is active. The ablations show that both channels contribute and the pulse channel dominates: with pulses suppressed while in charge the five v11 centres crash 62–93 times under the primary hit (opponent 60), i.e. the advantage over the opponent disappears entirely; with set-points zeroed they crash 33–54 (7–22 more than the full centres, and they fail the manoeuvre, Δ +1.3 … +2.0). Because each ablation changes the whole manoeuvre and not only the post-hit reaction, it is the "more pulses after the hit" measure that ties the effect to the reaction. The pulses-on opponent (5 crashes) shows that a pulse channel during the segments is sufficient in the classical stack as well. The consistent reading is that robustness to mid-manoeuvre hits on this vehicle comes from keeping the wingbeat-phase-locked pulse channel active while the attitude is commanded away from hover. The learned layer discovered and kept that behaviour: it fires more after a hit, and the imitation-only networks already have it even though their teacher never pulsed during a segment (the pulse label was recorded after suppression), so the behaviour is generalised from hover rather than copied. But the classical phase-locked law with its pulses kept on is substantially more robust (5 vs 22–40 crashes; the SNN seeds trail it by +1.1 … +2.0 in paired Δ on the primary set), except under the ×1.5 sustained disturbance (5–9 vs 24): the learned layer re-discovered the mechanism and implements it worse than the phase-locked law (3-tick spike-triggered twitches against phase-locked windows). Fig. R6 shows one case per task in which the opponent crashes and the v11 seed-4 centre completes the manoeuvre; it is illustrative, not representative.

### 3.4 Limitations

- **The preregistered opponent was mis-specified.** Its pulse suppression during segments, a design choice made when the schedules were tuned, is the main reason it crashes under hits; the pulses-on variant found after review is better than it in every scenario, nominally included (0.955). All preregistered criteria and H1 verdicts are relative to the weaker opponent. The pulses-on opponent was never re-tuned (its schedules were tuned with pulses off), so the best classical design on this vehicle is still unknown and could be stronger. The learned layer has not been shown to beat it: it matches the preregistered opponent nominally, trails classical + ES by 0.02–0.05 and the pulses-on opponent by 0.09–0.12 (paired); the pulses-on opponent's nominal gain over the preregistered opponent is mostly a redirect effect (per-task Δ −0.08 / −0.27 / −0.05), the task on which the SNN's own nominal gain lies (−0.12 … −0.19), so the two plausibly share the mechanism.
- **The positive claim is bounded.** The SNN's advantage over the preregistered opponent vanishes at 4.5 mN·m and under double hits for most seeds, is absent under plant mismatch, and one seed fails on roll-axis hits that the opponent survives in 192/192 cases.
- **The mechanism is established by ablation and by a classical counterpart, not by the preregistered measure**, which failed; the "more pulses after the hit" measure had no preregistered pass criterion, and the ablations remove a channel for the whole manoeuvre rather than only for the reaction.
- **Statistics.** 192 cases give ±0.05 on Δ, so "parity" means |Δ| ≲ 0.05 undetectable; 15 descriptive scenarios × 5 seeds were tested without correction; H1 is degenerate at both ends of the crash range.
- **Seeds and machines.** The five confirmatory seeds share five imitation starts and one teacher; five further SNN seeds and two upper-bound seeds come from a machine that does not reproduce the reference bit for bit and their rows are not archived locally.
- **Scope.** One simulated vehicle; idealised disturbance and sensor models; pulses give pitch torque only, and the dash roll class remains the weakest cell for every learned controller. The ratio criterion of the preregistrations was unreachable for every controller trained, and the paired statistic was introduced by amendment before the v11 results and after the v7–v10 ones. The plant-mismatch scenario was demoted after its result was seen.
