    Import your robot¶

    FlapSim loads URDF and MJCF through MuJoCo's MjSpec. What those formats cannot say about a flapping robot goes into a manifest, robot.yaml, next to the model file.

    1. Inspect the model¶

    flapsim inspect my_robot.urdf

    prints every body with its parent and mass, every joint with its type, axis and range, and the sites. Note the wing bodies, the stroke joints and the passive feathering joints.

    2. Write the manifest¶

    schema: flapsim.robot/1
name: MyFlapper
aliases: [my_flapper]

model:
  file: urdf/my_robot.urdf        # relative to this file; .xml for MJCF
  root_body: base_link            # a direct child of the world body
  base: {type: fixed, position: [0.0, 0.0, 0.5]}
  timestep: 5.0e-5
  integrator: implicitfast
  patches:                        # what URDF cannot express
    joints:
      left_feathering:  {stiffness: 2.0e-7, armature: 1.0e-10,
                         solref_limit: [4.0e-4, 1.0]}
      right_feathering: {stiffness: 2.0e-7, armature: 1.0e-10,
                         solref_limit: [4.0e-4, 1.0]}
    equalities:
      - {type: joint, targets: [left_feathering, right_feathering], polycoef: [0, 1]}
    bodies:
      base_link: {when: free, mass: 0.03, com: [0, 0, 0],
                  fullinertia: [5.0e-5, 5.0e-5, 2.0e-5, 0, 0, 0]}
    exclude_self_collision: true

torso: {body: torso}              # body whose velocity the aero models use
air_density: 1.225

wings:
  - name: left
    body: left_wing
    side: left
    frame:
      span: [0.0, 0.0, -1.0]      # root -> tip, along the feathering axis (body frame)
      chord: [0.0, -1.0, 0.0]     # leading edge (hinge) -> trailing edge
    stroke_joint: {name: left_stroke, sign: 1}
    feathering_joint: {name: left_feathering, sign: 1}
    planform: {profile: rectangular, length: 0.07, mean_chord: 0.02}
    mass_model: {type: spar_plate, mass: 6.26e-5, spar_fraction: 0.8}
    shoulder_offset: 0.014
  - name: right
    body: right_wing
    side: right
    mirror_of: left               # shares the left frame at the neutral pose
    stroke_joint: {name: right_stroke, sign: -1}
    feathering_joint: {name: right_feathering, sign: 1}
    planform: {profile: rectangular, length: 0.07, mean_chord: 0.02}
    mass_model: {type: spar_plate, mass: 6.26e-5, spar_fraction: 0.8}
    shoulder_offset: 0.014

    Wing frame¶

    span points from the wing root to the tip along the feathering axis; chord points from the leading edge (the hinge line) towards the trailing edge. Both are unit-free vectors in the wing body's frame. FlapSim derives the surface normal, so the side of the hinge on which the plate lies is never ambiguous. A site whose +X is the chord and +Y the span works too. A mirrored wing can simply say mirror_of: <other wing>.

    Joint signs¶

    Positive stroke angle is a right-handed rotation about the stroke axis (side * Z of the aero frame), positive feathering angle a right-handed rotation about +Y for both wings. sign converts the MuJoCo joint coordinate. If you get it wrong the loader warns:

    wing 'left': positive stroke_joint rate is not a right-handed rotation about
side * Z (the stroke axis); flip the joint's 'sign'.

    It also warns when the wing's centre of mass lies on the wrong side of the hinge for the chosen chord.

    Planform¶

    • rectangular or elliptic with length and mean_chord;
    • polyline: chord_polyline: [[r, c], ...] in metres from root to tip;
    • integrals: the eight blade-element integrals directly;
    • pitch_axis_fraction (hinge inside the chord) or leading_edge_offset (root spar between hinge and leading edge).

    flapsim.wings.from_polygon and from_mesh compute the integrals from an outline or an STL file.

    Mass model¶

    CAD exports often place a wing's centroid far behind the hinge. For a membrane wing that is unphysical, and it inverts the passive feathering phase (negative lift). Declare mass_model in wing coordinates (normal, span, chord), so mirrored wings share the same numbers: spar_plate (a leading-edge spar carrying spar_fraction of the mass plus a membrane plate), thin_plate, or explicit (com, diaginertia).

    3. Load and check¶

    from flapsim.robot import load

robot = load("path/to/robot.yaml")  # or base="free"
print(robot.wings, robot.geometry_warnings())

    flapsim inspect path/to/robot.yaml          # lists the resolved wings too

    4. Give it a setup file¶

    Copy sim.yaml from the example robot, change the joint names, and run flapsim run path/to/sim.yaml. Robots without CAD can start from flapsim.robot.flat_plate_flapper(), which builds a two-wing flat-plate robot and its manifest in Python.
